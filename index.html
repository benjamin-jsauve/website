Applied Research Scientist | Software Engineer

I study Mathematics and Computer Science at McGill in the Honours stream. It wasn't long before I felt inspired by the problem solving process I would find in a lot of my pure and applied mathematics courses. But math is kind of big. So I ended up taking a class in a few different fields, notably advanced statistics, probability, functional analysis, abstract algebra, graph theory and even some game theory. While some of my computer science classes weren't nearly as interesting and some we certainly outright boring at times, I did find my operating systems and algorithms classes rather engaging. And when you combine the two, things can get really fun.

Beyond my studies, I have done a few internships in quantitative research at proprietary trading firms and software development at big asset managers. I currently work as an applied research scientist at a quantitative hedge fund.

Before any of this, I worked as a professional chef in some of the better kitchens in Canada. Kitchens are a lot of fun, but the industry feeds off passion and energy. It's not really a place to make a living and cooking is more of a hobby than a passion, for me at least.

  1. 01research

    Peer Cluster Residual Momentum

    Portfolio Optimization · Russell 1000, 2005–2024

    Tests whether idiosyncratic momentum is sharper within data-driven peer groups. The pipeline residualises returns on the Fama–French five factors, cleans the covariance with Marchenko–Pastur, clusters stocks with Ward linkage, and trades the within-cluster signal through a Gârleanu–Pedersen optimiser with transaction costs. An optional random-forest meta-labeling layer learns when to size the bet up or down.

  2. 02merged

    Multi-quantile loss for EvoTrees.jl

    Open source · Gradient-boosted trees

    Added a :multiquantile loss to EvoTrees.jl, a gradient-boosted trees library in Julia, so a single EvoTreeRegressor predicts several quantiles at once. Leaf values use exact residual quantiles, split gain is summed across the K outputs, and each quantile gets its own bias initialisation. Runs on CPU and GPU.
